# Solver accuracy contracts

The [package entry point](../src/index.ts) exports the functions and types below. Use SI units and frequencies in Hz. Complex quantities use `exp(jωt)` with `ω = 2πf`. Voltage, current, pressure, and volume flow use RMS amplitudes. Excursion and particle speed are sinusoidal peaks. A positive input is finite and strictly greater than zero unless stated otherwise.

Most point solvers evaluate the stated equations in binary64. Lean proves selected properties of the exact real and complex equations. The independent regression tests compare selected implementations with higher-precision arithmetic, a separate circuit solve, numerical integration, or measurements. The comparisons establish accuracy for the tested cases. The per-call rounding and enclosure guarantees are stated below. [Lean proofs and replay](../verification/lean/README.md) · [Independent references](../src/highPrecision.test.ts)

Runtime certificate checkers recompute enclosures on a 160-bit fixed-point dyadic grid with outward rounding. Lean kernel replay checks a concrete certificate against an independently evaluated exact model using proved rational enclosures. The replay scripts encode finite JavaScript numbers as exact binary rationals. The exact-model proofs and certificate replays leave general TypeScript and JavaScript engine binary64 correspondence outside their scope.

Run `npm run verify:formal` to audit the Lean libraries and replay eight band, duct, and impedance-fit certificates from a clean install of the npm archive. [Verification commands](../verification/lean/README.md)

## Complex arithmetic and SPL

`Complex` stores the real and imaginary components of a phasor, with units inherited from the caller.

| Function | Equation and numerical contract |
| --- | --- |
| `c(re, im = 0)` | Store the two numbers directly. |
| `add(a, b)`, `sub(a, b)` | Componentwise binary64 sum or difference. |
| `mul(a, b)` | Evaluate `(ar·br − ai·bi, ai·br + ar·bi)` by ordinary binary64 operations. Intermediate rounding, overflow, and underflow can affect the result. |
| `magnitude(a)` | Evaluate `√(re² + im²)` with `Math.hypot`. |
| `div(a, b)` | Evaluate the exact component ratios `(ar·br + ai·bi, ai·br − ar·bi) / (br² + bi²)` from the input dyads and round each to nearest binary64 with ties to even, including subnormals. Throws `RangeError` for nonfinite inputs, a zero divisor, or a component that rounds to infinity. |
| `reciprocal(a)` | Equivalent to `div(c(1), a)`, with the same rounding and failure rules. |

The constructor, sum, difference, product, and magnitude perform no validation and follow JavaScript numeric behavior for nonfinite values. Circuit and filtered sweep SPL use the internal `pressureSplDb`, with exact formula `20 log10(max(|p|, 10⁻¹² Pa) / (20 µPa))`. The implementation scales the magnitude and subtracts logarithms to avoid overflow in the ratio. Reference tests check agreement within `2×10⁻¹²` dB at selected extreme magnitudes and near the pressure floor. Lean proves the rounding-neighbor, representative, and scaled-logarithm properties. ## Filters and DSP

`FilterTransfer` is a dimensionless complex voltage ratio. `Lr24Kind` is `highpass` or `lowpass`. `HighPassFilterType` is `LR24` or `BW24`. `BiquadCoefficients` has `kind: 'biquad'` and five dimensionless normalized coefficients `b0, b1, b2, a1, a2`, with `a0 = 1`. `DigitalFilterChain` supplies a sample rate in Hz and a stage list of `ParametricEq` specifications or normalized coefficients. `FilterChain` specifies optional analog cutoffs, a high-pass type (default LR24), gain in dB (default 0), delay in seconds (default 0), and a digital chain. The low-pass is LR24.

- `designBiquad(stage, sampleRateHz)` evaluates the normalized Audio EQ Cookbook bilinear-transform formulas. A `ParametricEq` specifies a peaking stage with positive Q or a low/high shelf with slope `S ∈ (0, 1]`. The stage frequency is positive and strictly below Nyquist and the dB gain is finite. For `A = 10^(gainDb/40)`, `θ = 2πf/fs`, `t = cos(θ)`, and `α = sin(θ)/(2Q)`, the unnormalized peaking numerator is `(1+αA, −2t, 1−αA)` and the denominator is `(1+α/A, −2t, 1−α/A)`. For a shelf define `u = A+1`, `v = A−1`, `e = sin(θ)√((A²+1)(1/S−1)+2A)`, and `σ = +1` for low shelf or `−1` for high shelf. The numerator is `A·(u−σvt+e, 2σ(v−σut), u−σvt−e)` and the denominator is `(u+σvt+e, −2σ(v+σut), u+σvt−e)`. Normalize both by the leading denominator coefficient. The returned binary64 coefficients must be finite and satisfy `|a2| < 1` and the runtime test `|a1| < 1 + a2`.
- `biquadTransfer(f, fs, coefficients)` evaluates `(b0 + b1/z + b2/z²)/(1 + a1/z + a2/z²)` with `z = exp(j2πf/fs)` and the binary64 coefficients taken as exact inputs. `fs` is positive and `f ∈ [0, fs/2]`. Each returned component has absolute error at most `2^-40 × max(1, |exact component|)`. The dyadic checker verifies that inequality for a representative of its enclosure, using endpoint formulas at DC and Nyquist. It throws `RangeError` if the coefficient or frequency tests fail or the enclosure cannot support the error bound.
- `lr24Transfer(f, cutoff, kind)` evaluates the squared second-order Butterworth low- or high-pass. With `r = f/cutoff`, its denominator is `(1 − r² + j√2 r)²`, and its numerator is `1` for low-pass or `r⁴` for high-pass. `lr24AttenuationDb` returns `−20 log10(1 + x⁴)`, with `x = r` for low-pass and `1/r` for high-pass.
- `bw24Transfer(f, cutoff)` evaluates the fourth-order Butterworth high-pass. With `s = jr`, the transfer is `s⁴/(s⁴ + a s³ + b s² + a s + 1)`, where `a = √(4 + 2√2)` and `b = 2 + √2`. `bw24AttenuationDb` returns `−10 log10(1 + r⁻⁸)`. Both analog families require positive frequencies and cutoffs and use bounded ratios or logarithms for numerical range.
- `composeFilters(f, chain = {})` multiplies the enabled analog and digital transfers by `10^(gainDb/20) exp(−j2πf·delaySeconds)`. Supply positive frequency, a valid high-pass type, finite gain, finite nonnegative delay, and a positive digital sample rate when present. Digital evaluation includes Nyquist and requires valid stages. Zero gain from a filter null or numerical underflow can produce a zero transfer.

Invalid settings for these functions throw `RangeError`. Coefficient design also throws when the rounded coefficients fail validation, and composition throws for nonfinite output. Coefficient design, analog evaluation, and the cascade use binary64 formulas. The per-component bound applies to individual `biquadTransfer` evaluations. The 320-bit reference tests check selected analog responses across frequency ratios `10⁻⁷` to `10⁷`, representative EQ designs, and a mixed cascade. Lean proves the exact stability predicate, centered transfer and endpoint identities, analog magnitude formulas, and delay and cascade magnitude properties. ## Small-signal circuit and passive radiators

`ThieleSmallDriver` supplies positive `fsHz`, `vasM3`, `qes`, `qms`, `reOhms`, and `sdM2`, and finite nonnegative `leHenries`. Q is dimensionless. The medium supplies positive density in kg/m³ and sound speed in m/s.

`CircuitInput` specifies the driver, medium, positive net acoustic compliance volume and terminal RMS reference voltage, driver count `n` and series string length `s` (positive safe integers, default 1, with `n/s` integer), and positive leakage and absorption Q or `Infinity` to disable a loss. The enclosure is sealed with neither branch, vented with a `port`, or passive-radiator loaded. A port requires positive Q or `Infinity`. A distributed path takes precedence over lumped dimensions. A lumped port needs positive total area and effective length including corrections. Radiation uses positive distance and solid angle, defaulting to 1 m and `2π` steradians.

`createCircuit(input)` snapshots the input and returns a function on positive frequency. Define `Cbox = V/(ρc²)`, `Cms = Vas/(ρc² Sd²)`, `Mms = 1/(ωs² Cms)`, `Rms = ωs Mms/Qms`, and `Bl² = ωs Mms Re/Qes`, where `ωs = 2πfs`. The exact RMS balances for one driver and the common cavity are

```text
Ze = Re + jωLe                           Zm = Rms + j(ωMms − 1/(ωCms))
Vterminal/s = Ze i + Bl v                Bl i = Zm v + Sd pbox
n Sd v = (jω Cbox + Gleak + Gabs + Ybranch) pbox
Iterminal = (n/s) i                      Zterminal = Vterminal/Iterminal
```

For a lumped vent, `Ybranch = 1/(Rp + jωMp)`, with `Mp = ρ Leff/Atotal`, `ω0 = 1/√(Mp Cbox)`, and `Rp = ω0 Mp/Qport`. A distributed vent uses the transfer below and uses its DC inertance for `Mp`. A radiator branch has `Ybranch = nr·Sr²/Zr`, with `Zr = Rr + j(ωMr − 1/(ωCr))`. The sealed branch is zero. Each loss conductance is `1/(Q Zchar)`, with `Zchar = √(Mp/Cbox)` for a vent, `√(Mr/(nr·Sr²·Cbox))` for radiators, or `1/(ωsealed Cbox)` for sealed. The solver clears the cavity and radiator admittance denominators. The point evaluation of the distributed branch uses the duct pole clamp described below.

`CircuitPoint` reports frequency, terminal complex impedance in Ω and current in A, and outward cone and mouth flow in m³/s. A radiator installation adds summed outward radiator flow and individual peak excursion. Pressure in Pa is `jρω/(solidAngle·distance)` times the sum of outward cone, mouth, radiator, and leakage flows. Absorption contributes to cavity loss. Port and radiator cavity flows have the opposite sign from their outward radiation. Pressure has no propagation delay. Cone peak displacement is `√2|v|/ω`, with radiator displacement computed from the per-radiator velocity the same way. Port speed is `√2` times RMS throat-flow magnitude times the spatial speed ratio (or `1/Atotal` for a lumped port). Mach is peak speed divided by sound speed. Terminal real power is `Vterminal Re(Iterminal)`, and total copper loss is `n Re |i|²`.

`RangeError` covers invalid input, mutually exclusive branches, bad frequency, unsupported derived motor or cavity values, duct evaluation errors, a failed complex division, or nonfinite results. The model is linear, lumped at the cavity, and uses additive monopole radiation. Thermal compression, nonlinear excursion, panel radiation, diffraction, and propagation remain outside the model. Lean proves balance, power, passivity under positive-resistance hypotheses, wiring, and the lossless radiator notch for the cleared equations. [Circuit implementation](../src/circuit.ts) · [Circuit proofs](../verification/lean/Electroacoustics/Circuit.lean) · [Radiator proofs](../verification/lean/Electroacoustics/Radiator.lean)

`PassiveRadiator` supplies positive moving mass in kg per radiator (including modeled added or air mass), suspension compliance in m/N, area in m², finite nonnegative mechanical resistance in N·s/m, and optional positive safe-integer quantity (default 1). Define `Kr = 1/Cr` and `K = Kr + nr Sr² ρc²/V` for the tuning formulas.

| Function | Exact target and failure |
| --- | --- |
| `passiveRadiatorResonanceHz` | Free suspension `1/(2π√(Mr Cr))`. Rejects invalid radiator values or a nonfinite result. |
| `passiveRadiatorTuningHz` | Lossless fixed-active-cone tuning `√(K/Mr)/(2π)`. Rejects invalid radiator, volume, or medium, or nonpositive/nonfinite stiffness or result. |
| `passiveRadiatorMassForTuning` | Moving mass `K/(2π targetHz)²` and signed difference from the supplied moving mass, both per radiator. Requires a positive target and applies the same stiffness checks and positive finite result check to the new mass. |

These formulas use binary64. The exact mass inverse has a Lean round-trip identity. A negative mass difference calls for removing mass. [Radiator implementation](../src/passiveRadiator.ts) · [Design proofs](../verification/lean/Electroacoustics/Design.lean)

## Distributed ducts

`DuctSection` defines length in m, inlet and outlet area in m², and the profile. Length is in `[10⁻⁹, 1000]`, each area in `[10⁻¹⁰, 10⁴]`, and the outlet-to-inlet area ratio in `[10⁻⁸, 10⁸]`. A straight section has equal endpoint areas. In a conical section `√S(x)` varies linearly and in an exponential section it varies exponentially. `DuctPath` contains 1–128 sections ordered throat to mouth and 1–10,000 identical parallel ducts (integer `count`). Each mouth correction is in `[0, 1000]` m. At most 128 signed junction inertance lengths in `[−1000, 1000]` m may be lumped at the inlet, with positive total effective inertance. The optional dimensionless inertance and compliance factors are in `[10⁻³, 10³]` and default to one. They scale section density and compliance, with `k = (ω/c)√(Fi Fc)`. Corrections retain the unscaled density.

Each rigid-wall section solves the Webster equation `p″ + (S′/S)p′ + k²p = 0`, with `U = −S p′/(jωρFi)`. The state `(p, U)` is matched at interfaces, including area jumps. The cascade maps the downstream state to the inlet through `[[a, jb], [jc, d]]`. With pressure-release termination plus mouth inertance `Xout = ωρ Lout/Sout`, set `D = d − c Xout` and `N = a Xout + b + Xin D`, where `Xin = ωρ (Lin + Σ Lj)/Sin`. Here the matrix entry `c` is distinct from sound speed. This model includes evanescent and cutoff sections. Distributed wall damping, radiation resistance, bend loss, directivity, and transverse modes remain outside its scope.

| Function | Exact target and point-evaluation contract |
| --- | --- |
| `ductPathEquivalentLength` | Per-duct length `Lin + Σ Lj + Lout·Sin/Sout + Fi·Sin·Σ∫dx/S(x)`. Parallel count does not change it. |
| `ductPathImpedance` | `R + jN/(count·D)` in Pa·s/m³ and total mouth/throat flow ratio `1/D`. The optional resistance is finite nonnegative, defaults to zero, and applies to the whole parallel branch. The point solver clamps `abs(D) < 10⁻¹⁴` to that magnitude, retaining a negative sign when `D < 0`. |
| `ductPathTuningResidual` | Evaluate `F(f, V) = (ωV/c²) N − count·D` with density set to 1. The exact residual is affine in volume. Its continuous extension at DC is `−count`. |
| `ductPathTuning` | Estimate the first positive root of `F(f,V)=0` away from a duct pole. Uses the inertance estimate and acoustic length to choose a scan step, searches at most 4096 steps up to 1 MHz, then bisects 70 times. The floating-point scan supplies no all-earlier-frequency exclusion. Failure to bracket throws `RangeError`. |
| `ductPathPeakVelocityRatio` | Estimate `max over sections and positions abs(U(x))/(S(x)·abs(Uin,total))` in m⁻². Samples both endpoints and searches sampled interior maxima with local refinement. Uses the same pole clamp as impedance. Throws `RangeError` above 4096 subdivisions per section or 16384 total sampled positions, or on nonfinite output. |

Impedance and both velocity APIs take frequency in `[10⁻⁶, 10⁶]` Hz, sound speed in `[1, 10⁶]` m/s, and density in `[10⁻⁶, 10⁶]` kg/m³. The residual uses the same frequency and speed ranges and accepts volume `[0, 10⁹]` m³. Both tuning APIs require volume `[10⁻¹², 10⁹]` m³ and the same speed range. These validators throw `RangeError` outside their domains. Point transfer evaluation also rejects nonfinite matrix terms or nonfinite impedance/flow outputs. The residual’s final arithmetic has no separate finiteness check. The point formulas and searches use binary64. The independent duct integration tests exercise corrected tapered sections across cutoff and on both sides of a pole. Lean proves exact transfer determinants, conservation, DC inertance identities, and first-root and peak properties under stated hypotheses. The uniform open-duct formulas have a pre-pole uniqueness and mouth-peak proof. [Point solvers](../src/ductAcoustics.ts) · [Duct transfer proofs](../verification/lean/Electroacoustics/DuctTransfer.lean) · [Duct enclosure proofs](../verification/lean/Electroacoustics/DuctInterval.lean)

### Certified tuning and peak velocity

`DuctBoundOptions` supplies `relativeTolerance ∈ (0, 0.1)` and a safe-integer `maxSplits ∈ [0, 8192]` (default 4096). Malformed options containers throw `TypeError`. Invalid tolerance or budget throws `RangeError` when settings are reached. Tuning obtains its point estimate first and translates an unsuccessful scan into `unresolved`.

- `boundDuctPathTuning` returns a certificate for the first positive root in `[lower, upper]` Hz, with `upper − lower ≤ relativeTolerance·lower` (default `10⁻⁵`). The lower residual is strictly negative, the upper residual is nonnegative, and the bracket excludes zero in `D`. The certificate covers `[0, lower]` with spans where the residual is strictly negative. This certifies the first-root bracket and allows more than one root inside it.
- `boundDuctPathPeakVelocityRatio` returns a spatial witness and complete per-section position covers for the same relative-width inequality (default `0.001`). The exact peak uses the unclamped denominator at the requested frequency. The witness establishes the lower bound and the covers establish the upper bound, including interior antinodes and both sides of area jumps.
- `DuctTuningCertificate` contains version 1, the serialized modeled request and effective options, the bounds, tolerance, and ordered `earlierSpans` in Hz. `DuctPeakVelocityCertificate` contains the same version, request, bounds and tolerance, a witness with zero-based throat-to-mouth section index and position fraction, and `sectionSpans` covering the fraction interval `[0, 1]` for each section. `verifyDuctTuningCertificate` and `verifyDuctPeakVelocityCertificate` recompute the respective inequalities, validate coverage, limits and request binding, and return `false` for bad requests, malformed certificates, or any enclosure/check failure.
- `DuctBound<C>` is `resolved` with the checked endpoints, split count, and certificate, or `unresolved` with split count and reason. An unresolved duct bound carries no certificate or numeric bounds.

| Duct reason | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `bracket` | Tuning scan failed or the proposed positive bracket did not meet the endpoint sign checks. |
| `pole` | The tuning bracket or velocity frequency enclosure for `D` includes zero. |
| `numeric-range` | Interval evaluation could not complete within its numerical support. |
| `subdivision-budget` | Earlier-root exclusion or the spatial width test could not be completed within the refinement budget or available resolution. |
| `certificate-rejected` | The solver's candidate failed its replay check. |

## Alignment and enclosure sizing

These routines evaluate ideal acoustic design equations in binary64. Enclosure volume means acoustic compliance volume. Lumped vent lengths include end corrections, and area is the total of the parallel vents.

| Function | Equation and input contract |
| --- | --- |
| `sealedAlignment(driver, V)` | `SealedAlignmentDriver` supplies positive `fsHz`, `vasM3`, and `qts` and optional positive safe-integer quantity `n` (default 1). Requires positive volume. Computes `α = n Vas/V`, `fc = fs√(1+α)`, `Qtc = Qts√(1+α)`, and `f3 = fc√((a+√(a²+4))/2)` with `a = 1/Qtc² − 2`. The exact F3 is the ideal high-pass half-power point relative to the mass-controlled level. |
| `sealedVolumeForQtc(driver, targetQtc)` | Invert with `V = n Vas/((targetQtc/Qts)² − 1)`, requiring `targetQtc > Qts`, and return the alignment for that volume. |
| `ventTuningHz(area, V, Leff, c)` | `f = c/(2π)√(area/(V Leff))`, with positive inputs and result. |
| `ventLengthForTuning(area, V, f, c)` | `Leff = (area/V)(c/(2πf))²`, with positive inputs and result. |
| `ductVolumeForTuning(path, f, c)` | Solve the affine volume residual by evaluating at 0 and 1 m³ and taking `V = −F(f,0)/(F(f,1)−F(f,0))`. Requires positive slope and volume, duct residual and tuning domains, and a tuning estimate within relative `10⁻⁸` of the requested frequency. This acceptance check uses the point tuning solver. |

These APIs throw `RangeError` when a parameter, checked result, or branch test fails, including failures inherited from the duct solver.

`solveDuctLengthForTuning(input)` bisects the equation `ductPathTuning(pathForLength(L), volumeForLength(L), c) = targetHz`. `DuctLengthTuningInput` requires positive finite target, sound speed, endpoints in m, and tolerance in m (default `10⁻⁹`), ordered endpoints (equality allowed), valid callback outputs throughout, and a safe-integer iteration limit in `[1, 200]` (default 80). Invalid settings throw `RangeError`. Callback and duct solver errors propagate. A bisection guarantee for the exact model requires a continuous, antitone tuning function and valid endpoint comparisons against that function. The runtime checks the floating estimates and bracket width. The caller supplies the monotonicity assumption.

| Length status | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `root` | Retained bracket has length width at most `toleranceM`. Returns its midpoint and the point tuning there. |
| `shortest-tunes-low` | Lower endpoint tunes strictly below target. Returns that endpoint with the original bracket and zero steps. |
| `longest-tunes-high` | Upper endpoint tunes strictly above target. Returns that endpoint with the original bracket and zero steps. |
| `budget-exhausted` | Used the permitted bisections while the bracket remained wider than tolerance. Returns the current midpoint. |
| `resolution-limit` | Midpoint rounded to an endpoint before the width target was met. Returns that point and current bracket. |

`iterations` counts completed steps. Lean proves the exact design identities and the length bisection bracket result under the continuity and antitonicity assumptions. [Sizing implementation](../src/alignment.ts) · [Design proofs](../verification/lean/Electroacoustics/Design.lean)

## Sampled operating limits

`OperatingPoint` holds frequency, peak cone and optional peak radiator excursion in m, peak port velocity in m/s, Mach, terminal impedance magnitude in Ω, and optional reference SPL. `OperatingSource` adds per-driver RMS reference voltage, series driver count, parallel string count, per-driver DC resistance in Ω, peak `xmaxMeters`, and rated W per driver. Supply a nonempty sample list at strictly increasing positive frequencies, positive impedance magnitudes and wiring/reference values, integer wiring counts, finite nonnegative cone and port responses, and finite SPL when supplied. Cone and thermal ratings are active only when finite and positive. Radiator samples are checked when a radiator limit is requested.

`operatingSourceFromCircuit(input, points, limits)` copies the unfiltered circuit points into this representation, takes impedance magnitude, sets per-driver reference to terminal RMS voltage divided by series count, and sets parallel strings to quantity divided by series count. Validation occurs in the analyzers. If the analyzers apply a filter, supply points calculated at the unfiltered reference voltage.

Let `Vref` be the reference terminal RMS voltage, `P` the number of parallel strings, `Z` the sample impedance magnitude, and `G = |H(f)|` the filter gain. For any positive linear rating `L` and positive reference response `x`, the unity-DSP ceiling is `Vref·L/(xG)`. For the per-driver copper loss proxy the ceiling is `P Z √(ratedWatts/Re)/G`. Terminal amplifier voltage and current yield `Vmax/G` and `Imax Z/G`. These equations follow from linear scaling and `I²Re` for a driver in one string. A transfer of zero imposes no input ceiling at that sample.

- `analyzeOperatingDrive(source, wattsPerDriver, nominalOhmsPerDriver, attenuationDb)` takes positive nominal W per driver and nominal Ω per driver, plus a callback returning finite gain in dB at each sample. It computes per-driver voltage `√(W·Ω)` and terminal voltage as that value times series count. It scales the samples by the per-driver drive/reference ratio and `10^(dB/20)`. `OperatingScenario` gives the nominal drive and total nominal W, endpoint frequencies, and sampled maxima for excursion, speed, Mach, and per-driver coil loss. Each `OperatingMaximum` is `{ value, frequency }` in the metric's units and Hz, taking the first sample on tied maxima. The optional rating ratios are maximum excursion divided by Xmax and nominal W divided by rated W, or `null` if the rating is disabled. Invalid inputs, nonfinite callback gains, and nonfinite checked calculations throw `RangeError`. Callback exceptions propagate.
- `analyzeFilteredVoltageLimit(source, options)` reports the minimum finite unity-DSP voltage across the enabled cone, port-Mach, and thermal proxy constraints and across all samples. `VoltageLimitOptions` accepts the filter, optional positive Mach limit, and an optional positive RMS terminal reference within `4·Number.EPSILON·max(reference voltages)` of the source-derived reference. `VoltageLimitingFactor` names `excursion`, `port-mach`, or `thermal-proxy`. `FilteredVoltageLimit` reports the winning voltage, factor, frequency, SPL there when available, per-factor minimum candidates, evaluated range, and reference-drive filtered maxima for excursion, Mach, and coil loss. Invalid input, invalid filter, no active/effective finite constraint, nonpositive/nonfinite candidate, or nonfinite checked scaling throws `RangeError`.
- `analyzeOutputLimit(source, options = {})` calculates the per-sample terminal ceiling using the formulas with `G=1`, then divides by the actual gain for the input ceiling. `OutputLimitOptions` accepts a filter, optional positive Mach, amplifier RMS V, amplifier RMS A, and radiator peak m limits, and optional positive planned unity-DSP RMS drive. `OutputLimitingFactor` adds `amplifier-voltage`, `amplifier-current`, and `radiator-excursion` to the three speaker factors. Candidate ties retain the first encountered factor in the order excursion, Mach, thermal, voltage, current, radiator. `bandInputLimit` is the minimum available sampled input ceiling, first on ties. The point result gives peak permitted SPL by adding `20 log10(Vceiling/Vref)` to reference SPL, planned SPL by adding `20 log10(Vplanned G/Vref)`, and headroom `20 log10(VinputCeiling/Vplanned)`. A missing terminal constraint gives `null` terminal ceiling and factor. The input ceiling is `null` with no terminal constraint or at a transfer null. Derived fields are `null` when their prerequisites are missing, including maximum and planned SPL at a null. Invalid source, filter, reference, options, missing or invalid required radiator data, or nonpositive/nonfinite ceiling calculations throw `RangeError`.

The sampled analyzers use binary64 scaling and min/max operations. Lean proves the exact sampled feasibility and headroom equations. These are single-tone limits on the supplied grid. The copper-loss proxy is steady-state `I²Re`. Nominal watts, temperature, time constants, compression, and broadband duty cycles need separate interpretation. ## Continuous-band headroom

`analyzeBandOutputLimit(input, options)` encloses the greatest feasible unity-DSP RMS voltage over every frequency in the closed band. For each enabled constraint let `u_i(f)` be the reciprocal of its unity-DSP ceiling, including the DSP gain and using the square root of the power fraction for the thermal proxy. If `U = sup_f max_i u_i(f)` is positive, the exact ceiling is `1/U`. A span cover bounds utilization from above and provides a safe voltage. A lower utilization bound at one witness frequency provides an upper voltage beyond which a constraint is violated.

`BandOutputLimitOptions` uses the output options without planned drive and adds a positive increasing `minHz, maxHz` band, optional cone and thermal ratings (finite nonnegative, default 0 to disable), positive `toleranceDb` (default 0.1 dB), and safe-integer `maxSplits ∈ [0, 8192]` (default 2048). Other supplied limits are positive. A radiator limit requires a radiator. A Mach limit applies when there is a port. The circuit must pass construction validation and the whole band must lie at or below digital Nyquist when a digital chain is supplied. Invalid requests and filter validation errors throw `RangeError`.

The sealed, lumped vent, and passive-radiator equations use outward enclosures. The distributed branch clears the duct transfer denominator in the coupled solve and cancels the associated throat normalization in the Mach expression. This covers every axial position in each straight, conical, or exponential section, including corrected multi-section ducts and frequencies across duct impedance poles, subject to enclosure support and refinement. Delay drops out of the magnitude equations. Digital stages use their designed or supplied normalized binary64 coefficients as exact model inputs.

| `BandOutputLimit` status | Result contract |
| --- | --- |
| `resolved` | Positive `safeInputVoltageRms` and `upperInputVoltageRms` enclose the true ceiling. The conservative dB bound based on `20(upper/safe−1)/ln(10)` is at most `toleranceDb`. Includes a certificate, split count, empty unresolved spans, and `null` reason. |
| `unresolved` | Includes a split count, reason, and spans that still need tighter bounds (or the whole band for setup/replay failures). On budget or frequency-resolution stops, any returned safe voltage has been dyadically verified over the entire cover and any returned upper voltage has been dyadically verified at the witness. Either field may be `null`. An available `errorBoundDb` requires both and may exceed tolerance. No result certificate is returned. |
| `unbounded` | No applicable constraint or an identically zero DSP transfer. All numeric bound fields and reason are `null`, the unresolved list is empty, and the split count is zero. |

| Band reason | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `subdivision-budget` | The split budget ended before the gap target passed. |
| `frequency-resolution` | The next binary64 midpoint cannot be represented strictly inside its span. Takes precedence if the budget is also exhausted. |
| `numeric-range` | Filter gain or utilization evaluator setup exceeded enclosure support. An individual unbounded span enclosure can instead drive refinement to a budget or resolution stop. |
| `certificate-rejected` | The final candidate met the search gap test but failed dyadic replay. Its bounds are withheld. |

`BandOutputCertificate` contains version 1, the sorted-key serialized request (including explicit infinite Q values), ordered full-band spans, witness Hz, and the proposed RMS voltage and dB bounds. `verifyBandOutputCertificate(input, options, certificate)` returns `true` only if the request matches and dyadic replay checks the cover, witness, and gap within tolerance. Invalid requests, unsupported enclosures, and malformed or failing certificates return `false`. The solver runs the certificate check before returning `resolved`. Lean replay can verify the full certificate against the exact model. Partial bounds use the runtime dyadic check. ## Impedance measurement and fitting

`DriverMeasurementCondition` selects `free-air` (default), `added-mass` with positive kg, or `sealed-box` with positive acoustic m³. `createDriverImpedance(driver, medium, condition)` uses the motor parameters above and returns `Re + jωLe + Bl²/(Rms + j(ωM − K/ω))` in Ω. Free air uses `M=Mms, K=1/Cms`. Added mass increases `M`, and the test box multiplies `K` by `1+Vas/Vtest`. The original motor parameters stay fixed for the perturbation. Invalid driver, medium, derived motor, perturbation value, or frequency, unsupported division, or nonfinite output throws `RangeError`. The solver uses binary64 model evaluation with the complex quotient contract above.

`ImpedanceSample` pairs positive frequency with positive finite impedance magnitude or complex impedance of positive finite magnitude. `impedanceResiduals(samples, impedanceAt)` accepts 1–4096 samples in input order and evaluates each at its given frequency. Magnitude residuals use `δ=|Zmodel|−Zmeasured` and complex residuals use `δ=Zmodel−Zmeasured`. The output includes the original measurement, complex prediction, signed real or complex residual in Ω, and `|δ|/|Zmeasured|`. Invalid count, frequency, measurement magnitude, or nonfinite relative error throws `RangeError`. Model errors propagate.

The fitters minimize `Σ r_k²/(2m)` locally over a box, where `m` counts residual rows. A magnitude sample contributes signed `δ/|Zmeasured|` and a complex sample contributes two rows from the real and imaginary parts of that same normalization. Thus RMS is `√(Σ r_k²/m)`. Coordinates scale each physical bound interval to `[0, 1]`. The search uses finite-difference Jacobians, damped normal equations, projection to the box, and cost-decreasing accepted steps. The returned point depends on the initial guess and the search budget. The contract covers local diagnostics at that point. Global optimality, uniqueness, and uncertainty intervals are outside its scope.

- `ImpedanceFitOptions` sets a safe-integer iteration limit `[1, 500]` (default 100), tolerance in `(0, 1)` (default `10⁻⁹`) for RMS and projected gradient, and normalized step tolerance in `(0, 1)` (default equal to tolerance). Invalid options, unordered or nonfinite bounds, and initial values outside bounds throw `RangeError`. Sample and model validation failures also propagate.
- `fitDriverImpedance(options)` takes `DriverImpedanceFit` with initial driver, medium, free-air samples, and a known added-mass or sealed-box sweep. Each sweep needs at least four distinct frequencies and must meet the residual sample limits. It fits `fsHz, vasM3, qes, qms, reOhms, leHenries` in that order while holding `sdM2` fixed. Default bounds are one-quarter to four times the initial value, except inductance uses `[0, max(4·initialLe, 0.002 H)]`. Overrides need strictly positive lower bounds except inductance, which permits zero. Returns the fitted driver and residuals for both sweeps along with the fit diagnostics.
- `fitEnclosureImpedance(initial, samples, parameters, options = {})` holds the driver and unselected inputs fixed and fits a nonempty list of distinct `EnclosureFitParameter` selections with caller-provided positive finite bounds and finite initial values. The selections are `volumeM3`, `leakageQuality`, `absorptionQuality`, `portQuality`, `effectiveLengthM`, and `radiatorMassKg`. The two loss qualities affect impedance through their summed conductance and cannot be selected together. Port quality requires a vent, effective length a lumped vent, and radiator mass a radiator. The sample set needs more distinct frequencies than parameters. Invalid selections, missing geometry, insufficient frequencies, or failed bounds throw `RangeError`. Returns the fitted circuit input and residuals along with the fit diagnostics.

The diagnostics include iterations, active parameter bounds, RMS relative error, maximum projected gradient component, sensitivity rank, status, `converged`, and an optional certificate. For gradient `g = Jᵀr/m` of the normalized objective, the projected component is `|g_i|` in the interior, `max(0,−g_i)` on a lower bound, or `max(0,g_i)` on an upper bound. The local sensitivity test compares squared singular values of the normalized Jacobian with a floor, chosen as the larger of `m·10⁻¹⁴` and `10⁻¹²` times the estimated largest squared column norm. With a certificate, interval differentiation supplies exact-model RMS and gradient bounds. A full-rank witness uses a triangular preconditioner for strict diagonal dominance of the shifted Gram matrix. A weak witness bounds the Rayleigh quotient of a nonzero direction by the floor. If certification is inconclusive, `certificate` is `undefined` and the diagnostics use the floating residuals, a finite-difference gradient, and a pivoted QR rank estimate.

| `ImpedanceFitStatus` | Meaning in classification order |
| --- | --- |
| `poorly-identified` | Local sensitivity rank is below the number of fitted coordinates, even if RMS meets target. |
| `residual-target` | Full local sensitivity rank and RMS at or below tolerance. |
| `stationary` | Full rank, prior RMS test did not pass, and the interior point meets the projected-gradient tolerance. |
| `bound-constrained` | Same gradient test, with at least one active bound. |
| `stalled` | Full rank, neither tolerance test passed, and the search stopped before the iteration limit. The search can stop on a small projected step or its floating RMS test. |
| `iteration-limit` | Full rank, neither tolerance test passed, and the trial iteration budget was exhausted. |

Only `residual-target` and `stationary` set `converged`. A certified stopped status requires at least one decisive lower bound above tolerance, even if the other metric's interval crosses the threshold.

`ImpedanceFitCertificate` includes version 1, fit kind, canonical serialized request, physical parameter vector in fit order, status, outward bounds on RMS and projected gradient, sensitivity rank and floor, and the applicable preconditioner or weak direction. Use the bound fields when the error of a diagnostic matters. A floating RMS of zero can be retained when exact-model RMS is within tolerance, and the certificate still encloses the exact-model RMS.

`verifyDriverImpedanceFitCertificate(options, certificate)` and `verifyEnclosureImpedanceFitCertificate(initial, samples, parameters, certificate, options = {})` return `true` when the original request, endpoint bounds, interval diagnostics, witnesses, and runtime status checks agree. For `stalled` and `iteration-limit`, runtime replay reruns the search and checks the endpoint and stop condition. Invalid requests, unsupported enclosures, and malformed or failing certificates return `false`.

Lean kernel replay checks driver fits and sealed, lumped-vented, and passive-radiator enclosure fits. It proves feasibility of the fitted point, the exact residual and gradient bounds, the applicable sensitivity witness and status implications, and a first-order bound in feasible directions. For a weak witness Lean proves existence of a weak direction. For stopped statuses it checks the endpoint inequalities. Runtime replay supplies the search-history distinction and the reported deficient rank. Distributed-duct enclosure fits have runtime dyadic support, with Lean replay limited to the other enclosure classes. ## Adaptive sampling, sweeps, response summaries, and sensitivity

- `sampleAdaptive<T>(evaluate, metrics, options)` uses a log-frequency seed grid, both band endpoints, and in-range landmarks. `SamplingOptions` requires finite `0 < minHz < maxHz`, safe-integer `initialPoints ≥ 2` (default 48), safe-integer `maxPoints ∈ [initialPoints, 8192]` (default 768), and finite `relativeTolerance ∈ (0, 1]` (default 0.005). Landmark lists must be finite. Ordinary grid and landmarks must fit first. Reserved landmarks can then reduce regular seeds to fit. Near-duplicate interior seeds can be replaced by a landmark, with near equality tested at four machine epsilons of the larger frequency. The metrics callback must return a vector of constant nonzero length with finite components. Every probed interval tests the maximum across metrics of the midpoint departure from the endpoint average, normalized by `max(|left|, |mid|, |right|, 10⁻¹²)`. The midpoint is geometric, with an arithmetic fallback. Intervals exceeding tolerance split. Intervals without a representable interior probe are skipped. Returns all evaluations sorted by frequency and diagnostics with a count of queued unresolved intervals when the point budget ends. `converged` means no queued intervals remain. `maxEstimatedError` is the maximum accepted probe error or `Infinity` with unfinished intervals. This is a midpoint test and gives no uniform interpolation error over each interval. Invalid options or metrics, an overfull grid, or insufficient initial-grid resolution throw `RangeError`. Callback errors propagate.
- `referenceResonanceHz(input)` validates circuit construction and returns the lossless reference used to seed a grid. It is the sealed `fs√(1+n Vas/V)`, Helmholtz tuning for a lumped vent, the point fundamental estimate for a distributed vent, or the lossless fixed-cone radiator tuning. Its units are Hz and it carries the relevant circuit and tuning error conditions. It represents a model resonance, with measured and sampled peaks determined separately.
- `sweepCircuit(input, options)` takes `SweepOptions` (sampling options and optional filter) and samples the circuit using pressure real and imaginary parts, impedance magnitude, cone excursion, port speed, and radiator excursion as refinement metrics. It adds the driver and system reference resonances, applicable radiator free resonance, analog cutoffs, and parametric EQ center frequencies to the ordinary landmarks. The result includes the circuit points, adaptive diagnostics, resonance in Hz, and model metadata. DSP multiplies dependent phasors by `H`, peak responses by `|H|`, and powers by `|H|²`; impedance is drive-independent. The function recomputes SPL from filtered pressure. It propagates validation and circuit errors and throws `RangeError` for nonfinite filtered results.
- `massControlledSplDb(input)` returns the ideal inductance-free mass-controlled SPL reference. It uses the circuit convention and pressure `ρ Vterminal Bl Sd n/(s Re Mms solidAngle distance)` in Pa, then `20 log10(p/(20 µPa))` without the circuit pressure floor. Supply valid circuit driver, drive, medium, wiring, and radiation inputs for the model. This function checks its derived pressure and throws `RangeError` if that pressure is nonpositive or nonfinite.
- `responseGroupDelayMs(points)` accepts finite pressure phasors at positive, strictly increasing frequencies or the empty grid. It wraps adjacent phase differences into the principal interval and estimates `−1000 Δphase/(2π Δf)` in ms. At interior points it averages the two available slopes with weights proportional to the opposite interval widths. At an endpoint or beside a pressure null it uses the single available slope, or `null` if neither exists. Correct unwrapping assumes the actual phase advances by strictly less than `π` in magnitude between samples, with phase defined on the interval. Invalid phasors or ordering throw `RangeError`. The result is exact for linear phase in exact arithmetic under the unwrapping assumption, with the two-sided formula also exact for a quadratic phase at the interior point.
- `summarizeResponse(points, options)` takes `ResponsePoint` values (frequency, pressure, SPL, and complex impedance from `CircuitPoint`). `ResponseSummaryOptions` selects an optional closed band (default grid endpoints) and a `ResponseReference` of sampled `band-peak` or `explicit` finite SPL. It reports sampled SPL range and ripple, first minimum impedance magnitude and its frequency, and maximum defined group delay in the band. Each cutoff is the first upward crossing on the full grid of the reference minus 3, 6, or 10 dB, linearly interpolated in Hz and dB, or `null` if no interval crosses. Invalid response samples, reference, or band, including a band with no sample, throw `RangeError`.
- `analyzeSensitivity(input, variations, options)` takes `SensitivityOptions` with positive increasing endpoints in Hz and 3–1024 safe-integer logarithmic seed points (default 241). Each `SensitivityVariation` assigns one `SensitivityParameter` to a list of finite positive values, with zero allowed for `driver.leHenries`. Selections are `volumeM3`, any of the seven `driver.*` fields, `leakageQuality`, `absorptionQuality`, `port.areaM2`, `port.effectiveLengthM`, `port.quality`, and dimensionless `port.lengthScale`. Port selections require a vent. Distributed ducts use length scale (scaling section lengths, with corrections fixed) or port quality. Lumped ports can use area, effective length, length scale, or quality. Invalid settings, invalid scenario geometry, more than 64 variation records or 64 total values, or inherited circuit/tuning failures throw `RangeError`. The common grid adds every in-band lossless reference resonance. Returns the baseline and individual scenarios, sampled maxima and resonance deltas, ranking by maximum absolute sampled SPL delta, and pointwise envelope including baseline. This is one-at-a-time scenario analysis. The envelope covers only the evaluated scenarios at the sampled frequencies.

These APIs use binary64 point calculations. Lean checks exact midpoint error and seed properties, linear DSP scaling, phase and slope identities, sample summary bounds, and sensitivity envelopes and ordering.
